Popular Destinations in Lesotho

01

Maletsunyane Falls

Plunging 192 metres in a single drop, this is among the tallest single-drop waterfalls in Southern Africa. It spills into a sheer sandstone gorge near the village of Semonkong, "Place of Smoke", named for the mist that rises from its base.

Where
Semonkong, Maseru District, roughly two to three hours' drive from Maseru.
Do
Abseil beside the falls on one of the world's longest commercial abseils, ride a Basotho pony, or hike the gorge.
Best time
Summer rains (November to March) give the heaviest flow; winter brings frozen edges and clear skies.
02

Afriski Mountain Resort

At 3,222 metres in the Maluti Mountains, Afriski is the only ski resort in Southern Africa. Snow-sports season is short and special, and in warmer months the slopes turn to mountain-biking and hiking country.

Where
Mahlasela Pass, Butha-Buthe District, in northern Lesotho.
Do
Ski, snowboard or tube in winter; mountain-bike, quad-bike or hike in summer.
Best time
Skiing typically runs from about June to August, helped by snowmaking. Check conditions before you travel.
03

Sani Pass

Climbing steeply from KwaZulu-Natal into the Lesotho highlands, Sani Pass is one of Africa's most celebrated 4x4 routes. Its hairpin bends reward you with sweeping views and a summit pub often billed as the highest in Africa.

Where
On the border between South Africa and Lesotho; the summit sits at about 2,876 metres.
Getting there
A high-clearance 4x4 is required. Many visitors join a guided tour from the South African side. Carry your passport for the border post.
Best time
Spring to autumn is easiest. Snow can close the pass in winter.
04

Katse Dam

One of the highest dams in Africa, Katse is the centrepiece of the Lesotho Highlands Water Project. Its towering arch wall holds back a vast reservoir, set among remote highland valleys that are ideal for scenic drives and quiet exploration.

Where
Malibamatso River, Thaba-Tseka District, in the central highlands.
Do
Take the dam wall tour, visit the botanical garden at Katse, or enjoy the lakeside lodge setting and the drive in.
Good to know
The road in is spectacular but winding. Allow plenty of daylight and refuel beforehand.
05

Sehlabathebe National Park

A remote, peaceful corner of the Drakensberg, Sehlabathebe is part of the Maloti-Drakensberg UNESCO World Heritage Site. Expect wild grassland, rock arches and pools, and ancient San rock art, best explored slowly on foot.

Where
South-eastern Lesotho, near Qacha's Nek, on the South African border.
Do
Hike to rock art shelters and natural arches, go pony trekking, birdwatch, or fish in the park's streams.
Getting there
The roads are rough and the park is remote, so a 4x4 and good planning are advised. Light aircraft access is sometimes possible.
